Lab 4: Cleaning Web Data for Visualization

Airline Tweet Sentiment

This project uses 1,200 tweets sampled reproducibly from the public U.S. Airline Tweets dataset. The chart compares RoBERTa-estimated sentiment across six airlines.

Analysis

I used a reproducible sample of 1,200 public tweets about six U.S. airlines. In Python, I removed missing or duplicate tweet records, parsed dates, converted retweet counts to nonnegative integers, standardized whitespace, and retained the airline category. For TF-IDF, URLs, usernames, and numbers were normalized before tokenization, stop-word removal, and lemmatization. Sentiment was then estimated from the more lightly cleaned tweet text with the CardiffNLP Twitter RoBERTa model. The resulting labels and scores are model-generated estimates rather than ground-truth opinions. The stacked bars show the percentage of positive, neutral, and negative tweets within each airline, so airlines with different numbers of sampled tweets can be compared fairly. Negative sentiment is the largest category for five of the six airlines in this historical sample. Virgin America is the exception, with positive sentiment forming the largest share. These results describe this dataset and time period only and should not be interpreted as current airline performance.
